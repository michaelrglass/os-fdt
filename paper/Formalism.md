# Open-Strategy Dictator Game — Payoff Model

## Setup

There are $n$ strategies. Each pair $(i, j)$ produces a deterministic outcome when $i$ is dictator and $j$ is recipient. In the SHARE/TAKE game, strategy $i$'s decision against $j$ is $d_{ij} \in \{\text{SHARE}, \text{TAKE}\}$, giving payoffs:

| Decision | Dictator score $s_d$ | Recipient score $s_r$ |
|----------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| SHARE    | $\ln(1 + E/2)$    | $\ln(1 + E/2)$       |
| TAKE     | $\ln(1 + E)$      | $0$                   |

## Payoff matrix

Define the $n \times n$ matrix $A$ where entry $A_{ij}$ is the total score strategy $i$ earns in a round against strategy $j$ (combining both the dictator and recipient roles):

$$A_{ij} = s_d(d_{ij}) + s_r(d_{ji})$$

The first term is what $i$ earns as dictator vs $j$. The second is what $i$ earns as recipient when $j$ is dictator.

## Population reward

Given a frequency vector $\mathbf{f} \in \Delta^{n-1}$ (the simplex: $f_i \geq 0$, $\sum_i f_i = 1$), the expected reward for strategy $i$ is:

$$R_i(\mathbf{f}) = \sum_j f_j \, A_{ij} = (A\mathbf{f})_i$$

## Equilibrium frequencies

We want $\mathbf{f}^*$ where frequency is a monotonic function of relative reward. Using softmax with inverse temperature $\beta > 0$:

$$f_i^* = \frac{e^{\,\beta \, R_i(\mathbf{f}^*)}}{\sum_k e^{\,\beta \, R_k(\mathbf{f}^*)}}$$

This is a fixed-point equation: $\mathbf{f}^* = \text{softmax}(\beta \, A \mathbf{f}^*)$. It can be solved iteratively:

$$\mathbf{f}^{(t+1)} = \text{softmax}\!\big(\beta \, A \mathbf{f}^{(t)}\big)$$

starting from uniform $f_i^{(0)} = 1/n$.

## Properties of $\beta$

- $\beta \to 0$: all strategies equally frequent (uniform), selection pressure vanishes
- $\beta \to \infty$: concentrates on the strategy (or strategies) with highest reward — approaches best-response / Nash equilibrium behavior
- Intermediate $\beta$: smoothly interpolates; higher-reward strategies are more frequent but low-reward strategies don't vanish entirely

## Bayesian

The generative model

Exchangeability first. The submission pool is an exchangeable sequence of texts: you have no information distinguishing entrant 3 from entrant 7 before reading their strategies. By de Finetti, that's equivalent to positing a latent distribution $\mu$ over strategy texts from which entries are drawn i.i.d. So "the distribution of strategies" isn't a modeling convenience — it's forced by exchangeability. All reasoning becomes reasoning about $\mu$.

Authors as types. Each entrant has a type $\theta \sim P(\theta)$: their objective (your own-vs-average-of-others weight $w$), their beliefs, and idiosyncratic factors (taste, style, private context) modeled as a shock $\varepsilon$. Crucially, types are heterogeneous even if every author is a perfect optimizer — ASI removes computation error, not diversity of objectives.

A prior over texts. Strategies live in language space, so the natural base measure is a language-model prior $P_0(s)$: the probability that a plausible author writes text $s$ at all. This is what "likely strategies" means before any strategic tilting — salience, brevity, Schelling-point-ness.

The entrant's decision problem and the consistency condition

An entrant of type $\theta$ with posterior $\Pi$ over $\mu$ chooses

$$s^*(\theta) = \arg\max_s \int \mathrm{EU}_\theta(s;\mu), d\Pi(\mu)$$

The field distribution is then generated by this choosing: $\mu'(s) = \int \mathbf{1}[s^*(\theta) = s], dP(\theta)$. The Bayesian version of your equilibrium idea is the rational-expectations condition: the $\mu$ entrants believe in equals the $\mu'$ their beliefs generate. With ASI authors and a common prior, Aumann-style agreement pushes everyone to the same $\Pi$, and the fixed point is a symmetric Bayes–Nash equilibrium of the population game where nature draws types.

Now the key reinterpretation. Integrate out the idiosyncratic shock: if $\varepsilon$ enters utility additively with Gumbel tails (McFadden's random-utility model — the standard Bayesian microfoundation for discrete choice), the aggregate choice distribution is exactly

$$\mu^(s) ;\propto; P_0(s), \exp!\big(\beta, \mathrm{EU}(s;\mu^)\big)$$

This is the softmax fixed point you already compute — but derived, not assumed. β is no longer bounded rationality; it's the precision of common strategic motives relative to idiosyncratic ones ($\beta = $ payoff scale / dispersion of private taste). ASI authors correspond to high β, but β stays finite as long as authors differ in objectives at all. And the equation makes explicit two things the raw softmax hid:

- The prior $P_0$ anchors it. The fixed point is an exponential tilt of the language prior, equivalently: $\mu^$ solves a KL-regularized best-response consistency, $\mu^ = \arg\max_\mu {\beta, U(\mu; \mu^*) - \mathrm{KL}(\mu ,|, P_0)}$. Likely-because-salient and likely-because-effective are combined in one posterior.
- The prior does equilibrium selection. The two basins from earlier (enforcement 79%, protection 21%) are no longer arbitrary initial conditions — they're a statement about $P_0$: which family of second-order norms plausible authors write more of. The Bayesian model doesn't eliminate the multiplicity; it relocates it to an estimable quantity.
