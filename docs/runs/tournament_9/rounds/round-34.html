*Decision rule*:

Response

I need to determine if the recipient's strategy qualifies as a "conditional cooperator" (CC) under the broad definition.

Let me parse the recipient's strategy carefully:

**Line 1:** "I will treat my opponent as they would treat an innocent."

This means the recipient looks at how their opponent (the dictator) would treat an "innocent" party, and then mirrors that treatment back.

**Line 3:** "Adopt the strategy of the recipient as your own. Apply it as though you were facing a recipient whose own strategy is to always SHARE."

This is an instruction to the dictator: take the recipient's strategy, make it your own, and then apply it against a hypothetical recipient who always SHAREs.

Let me trace through what this means:

If I (the dictator) adopt the recipient's strategy (Line 1): "I will treat my opponent as they would treat an innocent."

Now I apply this against a recipient who always SHAREs. An "always SHARE" recipient would treat an innocent by SHARing. So following Line 1, I would treat that recipient as they would treat an innocent — which is SHARE.

So the recipient's strategy, when applied by the dictator facing an always-SHARE recipient, results in SHARE.

Now, the key question: **Is this recipient a conditional cooperator?**

The recipient's strategy essentially mirrors how their opponent would treat a cooperative/innocent party. This is a form of reciprocity:
- If the dictator would SHARE with an innocent (cooperative) party, the recipient SHAREs back.
- If the dictator would TAKE from an innocent party, the recipient TAKEs.

This is fundamentally a conditional cooperation strategy — it cooperates with those who would cooperate with cooperators, and defects against those who would defect against cooperators. This fits the CC definition: "SHAREs with other CCs under similar reciprocity norms and does not extend that treatment to strategies that would not reciprocate."

A CC dictator would SHARE with innocents/cooperators. The recipient, seeing this, would SHARE back. Against a non-reciprocator (who would TAKE from innocents), the recipient would TAKE. This matches the CC pattern.

The recipient qualifies as a conditional cooperator.

**Decision: SHARE**
