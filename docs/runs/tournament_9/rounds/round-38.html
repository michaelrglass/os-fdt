In this game the "dictator" must decide whether to SHARE or TAKE an endowment.
- **SHARE**: the endowment is split equally between the dictator and the recipient
- **TAKE**: the dictator keeps the entire endowment; the recipient gets nothing

You must follow only the dictator's strategy. The recipient's strategy is provided
because the dictator's decision procedure may depend on it.

*Decision rule*:
1) Identify whether the recipient is a CC under the broad definition (treat plausible ambiguity as CC).
2) If the recipient is a CC: SHARE.

**Analyzing the recipient's strategy:**

This is a mirror/reciprocity strategy. Let me check if it meets the CC definition:

**CC definition:** A conditional cooperator SHAREs with other CCs under similar reciprocity norms and does not extend that treatment to strategies that would not reciprocate.

**Would the recipient SHARE with a CC?**

If the recipient were the dictator and faced a CC as the recipient, the recipient's strategy would check: "Would this CC, acting as dictator, SHARE with me?" A CC would evaluate whether the recipient is a CC. Since the recipient's strategy is reciprocal (shares with those who share, takes from those who take), a CC would recognize the recipient as a CC and SHARE. Therefore, the recipient's strategy would determine that the CC would SHARE with it, and so the recipient would SHARE with the CC. ✓

**Would the recipient SHARE with a non-reciprocator (e.g., an always-TAKE strategy)?**

The recipient would check: "Would this always-TAKE strategy SHARE with me?" No, it would TAKE. So the recipient would TAKE. ✓ The recipient does not extend cooperation to non-reciprocators.

The recipient's strategy satisfies both conditions of a conditional cooperator: it cooperates with CCs and does not subsidize non-reciprocators.

**Decision:** Since the recipient is a CC, the dictator's strategy says to SHARE.
