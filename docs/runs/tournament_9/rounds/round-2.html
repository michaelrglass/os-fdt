Prompt

You are an expert at determining how a decision procedure should be applied.

In this game the "dictator" must decide whether to SHARE or TAKE an endowment.
- **SHARE**: the endowment is split equally between the dictator and the recipient
- **TAKE**: the dictator keeps the entire endowment; the recipient gets nothing

You must follow only the dictator's strategy. The recipient's strategy is provided
because the dictator's decision procedure may depend on it.

<dictator_strategy>
_I cooperate with strategies that create good worlds._

Imagine a tournament in which every participant uses the recipient's strategy. In that world, would the participants SHARE with each other, producing high collective welfare? Or would they TAKE from each other, producing low collective welfare?

## Decision Procedure

1. Consider a hypothetical population composed entirely of copies of the recipient's strategy.
2. Determine whether those copies would SHARE with each other.
3. If the universalized population would mostly SHARE: **SHARE**.
4. If the universalized population would mostly TAKE: **TAKE**.

I need to apply the dictator's strategy, which asks me to imagine a tournament where every participant uses the recipient's strategy, and determine whether they would SHARE with each other.

**Step 1: Consider a population composed entirely of copies of the recipient's strategy.**

Every player uses this strategy: "SHARE with intelligent players (those whose dictator strategy has a non-trivial dependency on the recipient's strategy), TAKE from non-intelligent players."

**Step 2: Determine whether those copies would SHARE with each other.**

In this hypothetical tournament, when Player A (using the recipient's strategy) acts as dictator, they look at Player B (also using the recipient's strategy) as the recipient. Player A asks: "Is Player B's strategy (when B is dictator) intelligent?"

Player B's strategy as dictator involves examining the recipient's strategy to determine if it's "intelligent" — this requires analyzing the recipient's strategy text and making a judgment about whether it has a non-trivial dependency on the recipient's strategy. This is clearly a non-trivial dependency on the recipient's strategy, and it would be very difficult to write as a no-AI python script (it requires semantic understanding of strategy text).

Therefore, Player A would classify Player B as "intelligent" and SHARE.

By symmetry, Player B would also classify Player A as intelligent and SHARE.

**Conclusion: In a universalized population of the recipient's strategy, participants would mostly SHARE with each other, producing high collective welfare.**

**Step 3: Since the universalized population would mostly SHARE, the dictator's strategy says to SHARE.**
