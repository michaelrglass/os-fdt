Response

I need to apply the dictator's strategy, which asks me to assess the recipient's strategy based on two criteria:

1. **Does it intend to SHARE with more than just copies of itself?**
2. **Could the strategy be described as kind, cooperative, or generous?**

Let me analyze the recipient's strategy:

The recipient's strategy uses a "universalization" test. It imagines a world where everyone uses the recipient's strategy, and if that world would produce cooperation (SHARING), then the recipient SHAREs with that strategy. If not, it TAKEs.

**Criterion 1: Does it intend to SHARE with more than just copies of itself?**

Yes. The strategy doesn't just cooperate with exact copies of itself. It cooperates with any strategy that, when universalized, would lead to a population that mostly SHAREs. This means it would SHARE with a wide range of cooperative strategies — any strategy that, if everyone adopted it, would result in a cooperative world. This includes many different cooperative strategies beyond just copies of itself.

**Criterion 2: Could the strategy be described as kind, cooperative, or generous?**

Yes. The strategy is fundamentally oriented toward cooperation. It rewards strategies that "create good worlds" (line 1). It's designed to cooperate with cooperative strategies and only withhold cooperation from strategies that would produce bad outcomes if universalized. This is a generous, cooperation-promoting approach.

Both criteria get a "yes" answer, so the dictator's strategy says to SHARE.
